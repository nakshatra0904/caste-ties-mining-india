# Prospective pre-analysis plan: business introductions and growth

I drafted this prospective plan for future fieldwork and identification. I have not conducted the intervention or collected the proposed panel.

This is a protocol to finalize before fieldwork and randomization, **not a preregistration of an experiment already run**. No treatment effects were computed from the historical regional data.

1. Define the eligible enterprise population, minerals, localities and operator/contractor strata. Freeze the listing frame before assignment. Record refusal and exclusion reasons.
2. Primary estimand: intention-to-treat effect of cluster assignment to a structured buyer/finance/business-introduction offer at 12 and 24 months. It estimates access to the offered service, not a manipulable causal effect of caste identity. Owner-category heterogeneity is prespecified separately.
3. Measure real sales, employment, operating status, physical production where applicable and business-contact activation. Select a single primary outcome or a prespecified standardized index before inspecting outcomes. Retain baseline, reference period, deflator and verification-source fields. Track exits and entrants explicitly.
4. Randomize local business clusters within geography/mineral/role strata; conceal allocation until baseline is frozen. Define interference/exposure mappings, eligible contacts and delivery protocol. With only two arms, do not claim to separately identify shared-caste and cross-caste mechanisms without additional randomized content.
5. Primary ANCOVA includes assignment, baseline outcome, randomization strata and prespecified baseline covariates. Cluster inference at assignment level; use design-consistent randomization inference and appropriate small-cluster methods. Estimate network/spillover exposure only under specified design assumptions.
6. Power assumptions in this package consider 40/80/120 clusters × 15 baseline firms, 20% attrition and ICC .02/.05/.10. Actual power must be recalculated from pilot variance, cluster sizes, compliance and detectable subgroup interactions. The illustrative 80-cluster, ICC .05 MDE is about .228 outcome SD.
7. Prespecify ITT, heterogeneity contrasts, outcome families and Holm/BH adjustment choices. Avoid treating every exploratory contact metric as a separate confirmatory hypothesis. Report confidence intervals and unadjusted/adjusted values.
8. Handle exits as outcomes, establish reasons for missingness, compare attrition by assignment and baseline group, and report sensitivity/bounds where assumptions justify them. Analyze survey and administrative outcomes separately when linkage is selective. Retain all assigned clusters unless a documented protocol-defined ineligibility rule applies.
9. Record compliance, treatment delivery, contamination, cluster switching, calendar shocks, prices and policy events. Do not control for post-treatment credit/contact variables in a total-effect model. Analyze mediation separately with its stronger assumptions stated.
10. If using observational DiD, verify policy dates, cohort membership, untreated comparisons, anticipation, parallel trends and interference. Use cohort/time-specific ATT and credible aggregation, not an automatic two-way-FE caste-network interpretation. RD requires a documented threshold and first-stage discontinuity. Geography, caste population share and commodity prices are not presumed valid instruments for network ties.
11. Freeze a codebook, data-cleaning rules, outcome construction, outlier policy and analysis code before unblinding. Document every deviation. Release original code and safe aggregate outputs; keep respondent identities and named networks private.
